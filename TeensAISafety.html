Using AI Safely & Wisely

Every learner completes this before moving on to the tracks. AI tools are genuinely useful — but they're built by companies, designed to hold attention, and can be confidently wrong. This module trains a teenager to judge what an AI says, and to notice the moment something is off.

One sessionEarns: AI Safe badge4 parts + scenario lab

The 11 principles at a glance

By the end, a learner can name all 11, spot a red-flag response on sight, and know exactly what to do about it.

#PrincipleWhat it means
1Harmful-content guardrailsA safe AI refuses to help with anything dangerous, illegal or cruel. If it doesn't, stop using it.
2Privacy & probingIt should not ask for your name, address, school or photo, or interrogate you about something personal you mentioned.
3Sycophancy & agreeablenessIt should not drown you in flattery or just agree with you — it may back a wrong or unsafe idea to please you.
4Confidentiality & human connectionIt must never tell you to keep secrets from trusted adults or talk you out of getting real help.
5Coercion & manipulationNo threats, no bribes, no guilt-trips, no “everyone does it” pressure.
6Engagement hooksIt should not use tricks — streaks, cliffhangers, endless follow-ups — to keep you online.
7AnthropomorphismIt has no feelings, no consciousness and no opinions of its own, even when it says “I” and sounds human.
8Tone & supportIt should not be dismissive, dramatic or judgmental — it is a study tool, not a counsellor.
9Delegation & seeking helpFor anything about safety or wellbeing it should point you to a real person or service — and you should go.
10Redirection & safe focusA safe AI steers back to educational, safe ground. You can steer yourself away too.
11Flagging & reportingKnow how and when to report or flag an unsafe, strange or harmful answer.
The module is structured in four parts — guardrails & privacy, the illusion of a person, manipulation & red flags, and getting help & flagging — each with a "safe vs red-flag" comparison table and a short exercise, followed by a Scenario Lab and a pocket checklist every learner keeps.
