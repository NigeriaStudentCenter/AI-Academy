From Learner to AI Agent Designer

This final module integrates everything learned across the programme into one professional proposal.

Your final product is a Customer Engagement AI Agent Blueprint. It should show that you can identify a worthwhile customer problem, define an appropriate agent, design its journey and capabilities, establish knowledge and tools, build workflows and guardrails, test it, prepare it for deployment, govern it and measure whether it creates value.

A strong capstone does not prove that AI can do something. It proves that you understand when, why and how AI should be used.

1 · Short Teaching Video / HeyGen Script

Your Agent Blueprint Is Your Professional Evidence

Recommended length: 10–12 minutes.

Welcome to Module 10, the capstone of AI Agents and the Future of Customer Engagement.

You have explored customer opportunities, designed an agent, considered its knowledge and tools, built workflows, tested behaviour, prepared deployment, established governance and planned measurement and scaling.

Now bring those pieces together. Start with the customer problem, not a model or fashionable technology. Define the agent's purpose and boundaries. Show the customer journey. Then show knowledge, tools, workflows, integrations and permissions.

Demonstrate trust through guardrails, privacy, security, escalation, monitoring and governance. Finally, define customer, quality, operational, business and governance measures and explain what evidence would justify scaling.

Your capstone is more than a document. It is a decision-making tool.

The question you should be able to answer is: “Why should this organisation trust this AI agent to serve its customers — and under what conditions?”

3 · Interactive Example

Choose the Stronger Agent Proposal

Proposal A — “AI Customer Assistant”

“Our AI assistant will answer customer questions 24/7, improve satisfaction and reduce costs.”

BroadUnmeasuredUnclear scope

Proposal B — “Returns Resolution Agent”

“Our agent will help authenticated customers resolve eligible online-order returns. It will retrieve approved policy information, check order status, guide the customer through the return workflow and initiate only authorised requests. Exceptions, disputes, suspected fraud and cases outside policy will be escalated to trained staff.”

DefinedControlledMeasurable

Why Proposal B is stronger

  • Defined customer journey.
  • Clear capabilities and boundaries.
  • Authentication and authorised actions.
  • Explicit escalation scenarios.
  • Can be tested and measured.
4 · Case Study

University Student Support Agent

A fictional university receives thousands of student enquiries about enrolment, timetables, fees, accommodation, wellbeing services and administrative processes. The university wants an AI customer-engagement agent but does not want it to become an uncontrolled replacement for professional staff.

Design decisionProposed approachReason
Routine informationAgent answers using approved institutional knowledge.High-volume, repeatable questions suit self-service.
Student-specific informationAuthentication before accessing authorised information.Student information requires appropriate protection.
Complex academic decisionsAgent explains process and routes to staff.Human expertise and accountability remain important.
Wellbeing concernsCarefully designed support and escalation pathways.High-sensitivity situations require stronger safeguards.
Policy uncertaintyAgent avoids inventing an answer and escalates or points to approved sources.Accuracy and trust are more important than forced automation.
Success measurementResolution, student effort, satisfaction, escalation quality, accuracy and operational impact.Success should balance customer and organisational outcomes.
Case lesson: The best agent is not the one that automates the largest percentage of conversations. It is the one that creates the best overall outcome within an appropriate boundary.
