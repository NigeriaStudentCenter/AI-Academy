Fact-Checking, Hallucinations & Data Safety

Intermediate~20 minAny AI · your judgement
What you'll be able to do: catch AI mistakes before they cost you, and know exactly what data you must never put into a public AI — the trust-and-safety skills every employer now expects.
The scenario: An AI-written report looks perfect — but one confident statistic is invented and a "source" doesn't exist. You almost sent it to a client. Meanwhile a colleague pastes customer data into a free chatbot, not realising the risk.
Use any free app: these prompts work in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot — pick whichever you like. Everything here works on the free version.

1Two habits that protect you

Trust, then verify: AI can state false things confidently ("hallucinate"). Always check facts, figures and sources.
Guard the data: never paste confidential, personal or client data into public AI tools.

2Force sources you can check

Make the AI show its working so you can verify.

For every factual claim or statistic in your answer, add a source I can check, and clearly flag anything you are NOT confident about. Question: [your question]
What good looks like: answers where claims carry checkable sources and uncertain points are flagged, not hidden.
Refine it: reply — "Which of these sources did you actually verify versus assume?"

3Run a fact-check pass

Turn the AI into a sceptical reviewer of a draft.

Act as a fact-checker. Review the text below and list any claims that may be inaccurate, outdated, or need a source before I share this. Text: [paste]
What good looks like: a short list of the specific claims to double-check before you publish.
Refine it: reply — "Rank them by how risky it would be to get each one wrong."

4Redact before you share

Strip sensitive data before anything goes near a public tool.

Rewrite the text below removing any confidential company data, client names, personal details or numbers I should not expose. Replace them with [REDACTED]. Text: [paste]
What good looks like: a safe version with sensitive items replaced, ready to use with a public AI.
Refine it: reply — "List what you redacted so I can double-check nothing was missed."

Never paste these into a public AI

  • Customer or client personal data (names, emails, addresses)
  • Financial data, contracts or trade secrets
  • Passwords, API keys or logins
  • Anything your employer's policy marks confidential

For sensitive work data, use an enterprise tool like Microsoft 365 Copilot, which keeps data inside your organisation.

Check your understanding

Q1. What is an AI "hallucination"?

When the AI states something false or invented as if it were true.

Q2. Name one thing you must never paste into a public AI tool.

Client/personal data, financial data, passwords or anything confidential.

Q3. Which type of tool is safer for sensitive company data?

An enterprise tool like Microsoft 365 Copilot that keeps data inside the organisation.
