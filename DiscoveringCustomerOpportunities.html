1 · Lesson Page

Don't Start With the AI. Start With the Customer.

The most common mistake in AI-agent projects is asking “Where can we use AI?” before asking “Where are customers experiencing unnecessary friction?”

Good agent design begins with discovery. Investigate the customer journey, identify pain points and repetitive work, understand the business outcome, and then decide whether an agent is appropriate.

What is the customer actually trying to accomplish? Focus on the desired outcome, not the internal process.

Find confusion, repetition, delays, unnecessary forms, repeated explanations and avoidable transfers.

Do not automatically treat every pain point as an AI problem. The cause may be policy, process, data or system design.

Balance customer and organisational value against accuracy, privacy, financial, legal and reputational risk.

Discovery Principle

AI should solve a meaningful problem

Weak: technology-first

“We have AI. Let's build a customer chatbot.”

This may create a clever system without solving an important customer problem.

Stronger: problem-first

“Customers repeatedly struggle to understand delivery status, and staff spend significant time answering the same questions.”

Now there is a problem worth investigating.

The discovery question is not “What can AI do?” It is “What customer outcome could be meaningfully improved, and is an AI agent an appropriate way to improve it?”
2 · Short Teaching Video / HeyGen Script

Finding the Right Problem

Recommended length: 6–8 minutes.

Welcome to Module 3 of the BSOE AI Academy.

In Module 2, you learned the anatomy of an AI agent: goal, context, knowledge, tools, actions, guardrails and human oversight.

Now we face an even more important question: What should we build?

A powerful AI agent solving the wrong problem is still a bad investment. So in this module, we start with the customer.

Think about a normal customer journey. A person has a need. They search for information. They contact an organisation. They provide details. They wait. They may be transferred to another department. They may repeat information. Eventually, the problem is resolved — or it isn't.

Our job is to find the friction. Friction can be waiting, repetition, confusion, unnecessary effort, poor hand-offs, lack of information or inconsistent service.

But not every friction point should be automated. Suppose customers are waiting three days because staff manually copy information between two systems. An AI agent might help coordinate information. But if the delay exists because an outdated policy requires three approvals, AI alone may not solve the root cause.

This is why we distinguish the symptom from the root cause.

Once we understand the problem, we can identify possible AI-agent opportunities. Perhaps the agent could answer routine questions, retrieve information, coordinate systems or prepare a case for a human employee.

Then ask four questions: Does it create customer value? Does it create organisational value? Is it technically feasible? And is the risk acceptable?

This module is about discovery, not building. By the end, you will have a prioritised opportunity that can become the foundation for your agent design in the next stage.

Start with the customer. Find the friction. Understand the cause. Then decide where AI belongs.

5 · Case Study

A University Admissions Journey

The customer

A prospective student wants to apply to a university and needs to understand requirements, deadlines, documents, fees and the application process.

The journey

Search → programme page → eligibility questions → application → document submission → status enquiry → admissions decision.

The friction

  • Requirements are difficult to interpret.
  • Applicants repeatedly ask whether documents arrived.
  • International applicants have additional questions.
  • Admissions staff answer repetitive enquiries.
  • Some cases involve complex individual circumstances.
OpportunityPotential agent roleRisk
Explain published requirementsAnswer from approved admissions knowledge.Low–medium; accuracy matters.
Check application statusRetrieve status for authenticated applicant.Medium; access controls required.
Tell applicant they definitely qualifyMake final eligibility judgement.High; not casually automated.
Handle unusual circumstancesCollect information and route to staff.Medium–high; judgement required.
Case lesson: The agent can support the journey without becoming the decision-maker.
