The walkthrough

Five steps, one ready-to-use interview brief.

Copy each prompt as-is, or adapt it — just fill in the brackets with your own details.

1
~2 minutes

Gather your inputs

Before you open any AI tool, have three things ready: the full job posting text, the company's official website URL, and a two-sentence summary of your own background. Specific inputs get you a specific brief — vague inputs get you generic filler.

Tip: paste the whole job posting, not just the title. The exact wording tells the AI what the company actually cares about for this role.
2
~5 minutes

Build the company brief

Use a tool with live web access — Perplexity and Copilot are strongest here because they cite sources by default; Gemini and ChatGPT work well too once browsing/search is switched on.

Prompt to copyI have an interview for [Job Title] at [Company Name]. Using current, cited sources, give me: 1. A 3-sentence summary of what the company does and who its customers are 2. Their 3 biggest news items from the last 6 months, with dates 3. Their main competitors 4. Any stated company values or mission language from their own website 5. One signal about their financial health or growth (funding round, revenue report, hiring trends, layoffs) Cite a source for each point.
Save this response — it's the foundation for everything else, and your first piece of evidence.
3
~3 minutes

Turn news into talking points

Raw headlines aren't talking points. Ask a follow-up question in the same conversation so the AI can build on the brief it just gave you.

Prompt to copyFrom the recent news items above, pick the 2 most relevant to a [Job Title] role. For each one, write a single sentence I could naturally say in an interview that shows I've been paying attention — not just reciting the headline.
You're aiming for something you could actually say out loud without sounding like you're reading a press release.
4
~3 minutes

Generate sharp questions to ask

Generic questions ("what's the culture like?") signal you didn't prepare. Specific questions signal the opposite.

Prompt to copyBased on everything above, write 5 interview questions I could ask that are specific to [Company Name] and this [Job Title] role — not generic questions. At least 2 should reference the recent news or challenges we found.
Pick your favourite 2–3 to actually ask. Firing off all 5 in one interview is overkill.
5
~2 minutes

Verify before you walk in

Even AI tools that cite sources can still get a date, a name or a number wrong. Before the interview, cross-check any single fact you plan to say out loud — a funding figure, a launch date, an executive's name — against the company's own website or a quick second search.

If a claim didn't come with a source, treat it as unverified. Don't repeat it to an interviewer as fact.
Prove-It

Your one-page interview brief: a company summary, 2 talking points, 3 questions to ask — plus one fact you verified independently.

Before you start

Frequently asked questions

Which AI tool should I actually use?

Perplexity and Copilot are the strongest choices here because they search the live web and cite sources by default. Gemini and ChatGPT work well too, as long as browsing or search is switched on — without it, they may rely on older training data.

What if the AI gets a fact wrong?

That's exactly what Step 5 is for. Treat any single fact you plan to say out loud as unverified until you've checked it against the company's own site or a second search.

Do I need a paid AI subscription?

No. The free tier of any of the four tools listed — Copilot, Gemini, Perplexity or ChatGPT — is enough to complete this lesson.

How is this different from just searching the company myself?

A search engine hands you a list of links to read one by one. This walkthrough has the AI synthesise across many sources into one structured brief, then generate tailored talking points and questions — the parts a search engine won't do for you.
