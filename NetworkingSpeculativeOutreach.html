Networking & Speculative Outreach

Beginner~15 minChatGPT · Claude · Copilot · LinkedIn
What you'll be able to do: write messages that actually get replies — to connect with people, ask for advice, and reach companies before a job is even advertised.
The scenario: Most jobs are filled through people, not job boards. But Tom hates "selling himself", so his messages sound awkward and get ignored. A warm, personalised, low-pressure message - drafted with AI - gets replies.
Use any free app: these prompts work in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot — pick whichever you like. Everything here works on the free version.

1Do a little homework first

Before messaging anyone, note one real thing about them or their company (a post, a project, a shared interest). Personalisation is what gets replies. Draft in any AI tool, send on LinkedIn.

2A connection request that gets accepted

Short, warm, and clearly personalised.

Write a short, warm LinkedIn connection message to [name], a [role] at [company]. Mention [the real thing you noticed - a post, project or shared interest]. No hard sell. Under 300 characters.
What a good result looks like: e.g. "Hi Ade, I loved your post on breaking into UX - I'm making that move myself and would value connecting."
Refine it: reply — "Make it a little more casual and end with an easy opening line."

3Ask for advice (not a job)

People love to help - if you make it easy and low-pressure.

Write a friendly message asking [name] for 15 minutes of advice about getting into [field]. Show I've researched them, keep it low-pressure and easy to say yes to. Do NOT ask for a job directly.
What a good result looks like: a short note that flatters genuinely, asks for a small, specific thing, and makes saying yes effortless.
Refine it: reply — "Offer a couple of specific times and a phone or video option."

4A speculative message to a company

Reach companies before they advertise.

Write a short speculative message to [company] expressing interest in [type of role], showing what I could bring based on my background [paste 2 lines]. Warm, confident, not desperate.
What a good result looks like: a brief, confident note that leads with value to them, not just what you want.
Refine it: reply — "Add one line about a specific thing they do that I admire."

Outreach etiquette

  • Always personalise - never copy-paste the same message
  • Keep it short; respect their time
  • Make it easy to say yes (small, specific ask)
  • Don't ask for a job outright in a first message
  • Follow up once, politely, if there's no reply
Try this today (5 min): find one person in a role you'd like and draft a personalised connection message with the Step 2 prompt. Personalise the AI's draft, then send it.
Common mistake: sending the same generic message to everyone. People spot copy-paste instantly and ignore it. One personal detail changes everything.
Privacy & ethics: be genuine and honest - don't fake a connection or claim things that aren't true. Keep messages professional and respectful.

Check your understanding

Q1. Why does most hiring happen through networking?

Many roles are filled through people and referrals before (or instead of) being advertised.

Q2. What single thing makes outreach get replies?

Genuine personalisation - one real detail about the person or company.

Q3. Should your first message ask for a job?

No - ask to connect or for advice; make it easy and low-pressure.
