Going Live Is Not the Finish Line

Once an AI agent is governed and operating safely, the next challenge is improvement. A mature organisation does not simply ask whether the agent is working. It asks whether customer outcomes are improving, whether the agent is reducing unnecessary effort, whether employees are being supported, whether risks are changing and whether expansion is justified by evidence.

The objective is not to make the agent do more. The objective is to make it create more value, safely and measurably.

The Agent Performance Scorecard

DimensionQuestions to askExample measures
CustomerDid the customer achieve the intended outcome?Resolution, satisfaction, repeat contact, customer effort.
QualityWas the response useful and appropriate?Accuracy, groundedness review, failure rate, escalation quality.
OperationsIs the service reliable?Latency, availability, tool errors, handoff completion.
BusinessIs it creating measurable organisational value?Cost-to-serve, conversion, retention, productivity.
Safety & governanceAre controls continuing to work?Policy exceptions, incidents, access events, high-risk escalations.
AdoptionAre people using the service appropriately?Eligible-user adoption, repeat usage, successful self-service.
Avoid vanity metrics. High conversation volume does not automatically mean customer value. A mature scorecard connects activity to outcomes.

Diagnosing a weak result

If resolution falls, do not immediately rewrite the prompt. First identify the cause. The problem might be missing knowledge, ambiguous customer journeys, poor retrieval, a failing tool, inadequate escalation, authentication friction or an unrealistic scope.

1 · Short Teaching Video / HeyGen Script

Measure Before You Scale

Recommended length: 9–11 minutes.

Welcome to Module 9 of the BSOE AI Academy.

Your agent is governed and live. What happens next?

The answer is not simply “add more features.” Mature AI operations begin with measurement.

Start with the customer. Did the customer actually achieve the outcome they wanted? Did the agent resolve the issue, reduce effort and provide a clear next step?

Then examine quality. Was the answer accurate? Was it grounded in approved knowledge? Did the agent know when to escalate?

Look at operations. Were tools available? Did the agent respond within an acceptable time? Did handoffs reach the right team?

Then connect performance to business value. Depending on the use case, that might mean lower service costs, faster resolution, higher conversion, improved retention or employee productivity.

Now comes optimisation. When a metric is weak, diagnose the underlying cause before changing the system. A prompt change will not fix missing source information or a broken workflow.

Use controlled experiments where possible. Make a defined change, identify the expected result, measure the effect and check that safety and quality have not deteriorated.

Finally, scale based on evidence. Expansion might mean more customers, another channel, a new language, additional knowledge sources or carefully controlled actions.

The mature mindset is simple: measure, diagnose, improve, validate — then scale.

3 · Interactive Example

Why Did Resolution Fall?

What the dashboard says

Customer resolution fell from 78% to 64% after a new service was introduced.

Bad response: immediately change the system prompt.

What a mature team investigates

  • Did the new service have complete knowledge coverage?
  • Did the connected tool fail more often?
  • Did customer requests become more complex?
  • Did escalation rules change?
  • Did authentication introduce friction?
EvidencePossible diagnosis
Resolution falls only for the new service.Likely capability or knowledge gap.
Tool errors rise at the same time.Integration reliability may be the cause.
Escalations rise but successful human resolution remains high.The agent may be appropriately identifying complex cases.
Customer complaints rise despite high resolution.Resolution alone may not capture quality or customer effort.
4 · Case Study

Telecom Customer Engagement Agent

A fictional telecom company has deployed an AI agent that handles billing questions, package information, appointment queries and selected service requests. After three months, leadership wants to expand the agent to additional customer journeys.

FindingEvidenceDecision
Billing FAQ resolution is high.Strong customer outcomes and low escalation.Consider expansion within this journey.
Appointment changes have frequent tool failures.Operational error logs show integration instability.Fix and retest before expanding.
Customers often ask questions outside the knowledge base.High unsupported-intent rate.Analyse demand before adding content.
Customer satisfaction is lower for complex complaints.Long conversations and late escalation.Improve escalation and handoff rather than forcing automation.
Cost-to-serve has decreased.Fewer routine contacts reach human teams.Useful business evidence, but not sufficient on its own.
Case lesson: Scaling is a portfolio decision. Expand what works, repair what fails, redesign what creates friction and keep human service where automation does not create a better outcome.
