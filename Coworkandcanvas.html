Lesson 4 — Cowork & Canvas: co-create with AI

Advanced~25 minDual track: no-code + code

Jargon, in plain words

New techy words for this lesson, explained like you're 12 — with when and why you'd use them.
Canvas
A side-by-side workspace where you and the AI build and edit something together — not just chat back and forth. Like sharing a whiteboard or a Google Doc with a colleague, instead of texting them every change. When: writing, editing, coding, planning. Why: you edit directly and improve it fast.
Artifact
The actual thing you're making in the canvas — a document, a web page, a chart, some code. The drawing on the whiteboard itself. (Claude calls these "Artifacts", ChatGPT "Canvas", Copilot "Pages".)
Cowork
Working alongside the AI as a partner, hands-on, rather than asking and waiting. Like cooking together in a kitchen, not ordering a takeaway.
Iteration
Improving something step by step, in rounds. Draft, tweak, tweak again — like polishing something until it shines. Why: the best work rarely comes out perfect first time.
Version
A saved snapshot of your work at a moment in time, so you can go back. Like save points in a game, or "undo".
Human-in-the-loop
A person stays in control — reviewing and steering the AI's changes. You're the driver; the AI is the very fast assistant. Why: catches mistakes before they ship.
What you'll be able to do: co-create a real deliverable with AI in a canvas — editing and iterating side by side instead of one-shot chatting — while keeping a human in the loop so the work stays right.
The scenario: Priya keeps re-typing "no, change just the second paragraph" into a chatbot, losing track of which version is which. In a canvas, she edits the document directly, asks the AI to adjust only that section, and refines it live. The polished version is done in minutes — and she never lost her place.

1What a canvas actually is

Normal chat is a back-and-forth of messages. A canvas opens the artifact — your document, page or code — right next to the chat, so you can cowork: edit it yourself, highlight a bit and say "improve this", and iterate round by round. You keep versions, and you stay in control as the human in the loop.

When it's used: polishing a report, building a landing page, refining code, shaping a plan. Why it's used: it's faster and clearer than re-prompting from scratch, and you can see and steer every change.

3Go deeper — build an interactive artifact (optional)

Skip if you like — you've already coworked above. To push it, ask a canvas to build something interactive and then iterate on it.

Go deeper · iterate a mini dashboard
  1. Ask: "Build a simple HTML dashboard showing these 4 numbers as cards: [your numbers]."
  2. Then iterate by steering: "Add a bar chart", "make the header green", "add a total at the top."
  3. Each request updates the same artifact and keeps a version — that's coworking on code.
Build a single-file HTML dashboard with 4 metric cards for these numbers:
[Sales 1200] [New customers 84] [Refunds 3] [Rating 4.6].
Then wait - I'll ask for changes one at a time.

You don't write the code — you direct it and review each version. That's the human-in-the-loop skill employers want.

Guardrails — safety & ethics

  • Review before you ship. Read every AI change — don't send an artifact you haven't checked.
  • Keep sensitive data out of anything you'll share or publish from a canvas.
  • Track versions and ownership — know what changed and who approved it.
  • You're the human in the loop: accept, reject or fix each change deliberately.
Try this today (5 min): open a canvas and co-build a one-page brief or a simple web page, editing it directly and iterating twice. Notice how much faster it is than re-prompting.
Common mistake: treating the canvas like normal chat — re-asking from scratch each time. The whole point is to edit and iterate on the same artifact.
