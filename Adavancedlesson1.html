Lesson 1 — Connectors & Context: give AI your real data

Advanced~30 minDual track: no-code + code

Jargon, in plain words

The techy words in this lesson, explained like you're 12. This glossary grows a little each lesson.
Connector
A safe link that lets an AI look at your real information — your files, your email, a database — without you copy-pasting it. Like giving a trusted helper a key to one cupboard, not the whole house. When: whenever you want the AI to answer from your own stuff. Why: real answers about your data, not generic web guesses.
Data source
Simply where your information lives — a Google Drive folder, a spreadsheet, your inbox, a database. Like the fridge, the cupboard, or the freezer where different food is kept.
Context / grounding
Giving the AI the real facts to base its answer on, so it talks about your situation. Like showing a tutor your actual homework before they help — otherwise they're guessing. Why: stops the AI making things up.
Permissions / access
What the AI is allowed to see. You decide. Like a visitor's pass that only opens certain doors.
Least privilege
A safety rule: only give access to exactly what's needed — nothing extra. One cupboard key, never the master key. Why: if something goes wrong, far less can be exposed.
MCP (Model Context Protocol)
A shared "plug shape" that lets different AIs connect to different data tools in the same standard way. Like a USB port — one standard plug fits loads of devices, so you don't need a different cable for everything.
API (you'll meet this properly in Lesson 2)
A way for two pieces of software to talk to each other and swap information. Like a waiter carrying your order to the kitchen and bringing food back.
What you'll be able to do: safely connect an AI to your own real data so it answers from your files instead of the generic web — and understand, in plain terms, how a connector (and the MCP standard) makes that happen.
The scenario: Amara spends every Monday copy-pasting last week's numbers from a shared drive, her inbox and a spreadsheet into an AI to write a report. It's slow and error-prone. A connector lets the AI read those data sources directly — so the report writes itself from live, correct information.

1What a connector actually is

Normally you paste text into an AI. A connector flips that around: it lets the AI reach into a data source you choose and read what it needs. You're not sending it everything — you're handing over one cupboard key. That's the idea of least privilege: give the smallest access that does the job.

When it's used: writing a report from your own spreadsheet, answering "what did this contract say?", or pulling last week's sales without hunting through folders. Why it's used: the AI's answer is grounded in your real facts, so it's accurate and specific — not a generic guess.

  1. In Gemini, type @ to see connected apps, then choose @Google Drive or @Gmail.
  2. Point it at a real file, e.g. "Using @Google Drive, summarise the sales figures in last month's report."
  3. Notice it answers from your document — that's grounding in action.

Go deeper · MCP (the "USB port" for AI data)

MCP is a standard way to plug an AI into a data tool — a folder, a database, a code repo. Instead of a custom link for every tool, MCP gives one standard "plug shape", so the same AI can connect to many things. Tools like Claude can add an MCP server that exposes, say, a local folder.

Conceptually, you tell the app which MCP server to run. A simplified example of the idea (a config that connects a local folder) looks like this:

{
  "mcpServers": {
    "my-files": {
      "command": "npx",
      "args": ["-y", "@modelcontextprotocol/server-filesystem", "/path/to/one/folder"]
    }
  }
}

Notice it points at one folder, not your whole drive — least privilege in practice. Always follow the official MCP documentation for the current, exact steps; the details change often.

Guardrails — safety & ethics (read this every time)

  • Least privilege: connect the one folder or file needed — never the whole drive.
  • Know where data goes: check whether the tool keeps or trains on your data; use enterprise tools for sensitive work.
  • No secret data by accident: a broad connector can expose HR, salary or client files you didn't mean to share.
  • Revoke access when you're done, and follow your organisation's data policy.
Try this today (5 min): connect an AI to one of your own files (a Drive doc or a OneDrive file) and ask it a question only that file can answer. You've just grounded an AI in your real data.
Common mistake: connecting far more than you need "to be safe". That's the opposite of safe — it exposes data. Connect the minimum.

Check your understanding

Q1. In plain words, what is a connector?

Q2. What does "least privilege" mean and why does it matter?

Give the AI access to only what it needs — one folder, not everything. If something goes wrong, far less data is exposed.

Q3. What problem does MCP solve?

It's a standard "plug" so many different AIs can connect to many different data tools the same way — like USB for AI data.
