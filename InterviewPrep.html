Interview Prep with an AI Coach

Beginner~20 minChatGPT · Gemini · Copilot · Claude
What you'll be able to do: run a realistic mock interview with an AI coach, get honest feedback on your answers, and walk into any interview prepared and calm — all on your own, as many times as you like.
The scenario: Tunde keeps getting interviews but freezes and rambles when he's in the room. He has no one to practise with and gets zero feedback after a rejection — so he never improves. An AI coach lets him rehearse unlimited times and get instant, specific feedback until he's sharp.
Use any free app: these prompts work in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot — pick whichever you like. Everything here works on the free version.

2Run a realistic mock interview

Paste this and answer each question before the next appears — just like the real thing.

Act as a hiring manager interviewing me for the role of [job title] in [industry / company]. Interview me by asking ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer before asking the next. Start with a warm-up, then move to competency and tricky questions. Ask 6 questions in total, then stop and wait — do not give feedback yet.
What a good result looks like: the AI asks one question, then genuinely waits — e.g. "Tell me about yourself." (stops) then, after you answer, "What attracted you to this role?"
Refine it: too easy? reply — "Ask tougher, competency-based questions and one about a weakness."

3Get honest, scored feedback

After the mock interview, ask the coach to mark you and show a stronger answer.

Now review my answers. For each question, give: - a score out of 10, - what was strong, - what was missing or waffly, - a tighter model answer using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Finish with the 3 things I should improve before a real interview.
What a good result looks like: "Answer 2: 6/10 — good example but no result. STAR version: '…which cut processing time by 30%.'" plus your top 3 fixes.
Refine it: want to go deeper? reply — "Re-interview me on just the questions I scored below 7."
STAR, in one line: answer competency questions as a mini-story — Situation (set the scene) · Task (what you had to do) · Action (what YOU did) · Result (the outcome, with a number if you can). It stops you rambling and proves impact.

4Prepare for the likely questions

Go in knowing what's coming and having smart questions to ask back.

List the 10 most likely interview questions for a [job title] role. For each, give me a 3-bullet answer framework I can personalise with my own examples. Then give me 3 strong questions to ASK the interviewer that show I researched [company].
What a good result looks like: a framework such as — "Why do you want this role? → motivation · a specific draw · how it fits your goals," and smart questions to ask back.
Refine it: reply — "Now role-play these 10 questions with me, one at a time."
Try this today (5 min): run the Step 2 prompt for one real role you'd apply to, and answer the first three questions out loud. Notice where you hesitate — that's exactly what to practise.
Common mistake: memorising answers word-for-word. You'll sound robotic and fall apart if the question is worded differently. Practise the structure (STAR), not a script.
Privacy & ethics: don't share confidential details about a current or past employer while practising. This is rehearsal — use general examples of your own work.

Check your understanding

Q1. What does STAR stand for, and why use it?

Situation, Task, Action, Result — it structures competency answers so you show impact without rambling.

Q2. Why practise with an AI coach instead of just reading tips?

You get to actually rehearse and receive specific feedback on your real answers, as many times as you need.

Q3. True or false: you should memorise your answers word-for-word.

False — practise the structure so you can adapt to how the question is actually asked.
