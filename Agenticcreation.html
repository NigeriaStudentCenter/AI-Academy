Customer expectations

Organisational pressure

More enquiries, more data and more channels create pressure to improve service while controlling workload and cost.

AI agents can combine conversation, knowledge, tools and authorised actions into a connected customer experience.

2 · Short teaching video / HeyGen script

Welcome to the Agentic Revolution

5–7 minute presenter script.

Welcome to AI Agents and the Future of Customer Engagement, from the BSOE Institute of Artificial Intelligence, Employability and Workforce Development.

In this course, we're exploring one of the most important developments in artificial intelligence: AI agents.

But we begin with the customer. Customers expect fast answers, personalised experiences and the ability to move between websites, apps, messaging and human support without starting again.

That creates a challenge: more customers, more channels, higher expectations, more data and pressure to control costs.

Chatbots could automate answers. Generative AI made conversations much more natural. AI agents introduce another idea: action.

Imagine a customer asks, “Where is my order?” A chatbot might direct them to a tracking page. An assistant might retrieve the information. An AI agent could potentially retrieve the order, check the delivery system and, within authorised permissions, take the next appropriate action.

An AI agent can be thought of as a digital worker. It has a goal, instructions, knowledge, approved tools, defined actions and boundaries.

Humans remain essential. AI can provide speed, scale and consistency. Humans provide empathy, judgement, relationships, creativity and accountability.

Our journey is Understand → Explore → Design → Build → Test → Deploy → Govern. By the end, you will have designed your own Customer Engagement AI Agent Blueprint.

Welcome to the agentic revolution. Let's begin.

4 · Interactive Example

Are you talking to an agent?

System A — Information

“Please visit our appointment page to make changes.”

System B — Conversation

“Thursday appointments are available. Would you like me to show you the times?”

System C — Assistance

“Thursday has three available times: 10am, 2pm and 4pm.”

System D — Agency

“I've moved your appointment to Thursday at 2pm and sent you a confirmation.”

Capability does not equal authority. An agent may be technically capable of doing something without being authorised to do it.
5 · Case Study

The Supermarket Customer Agent

The situation

A national supermarket receives thousands of enquiries about stock, opening times, deliveries, returns and complaints.

Proposed agent

It could answer store questions, retrieve approved product information, check order status and guide customers through returns.

Critical question

Should the agent automatically issue a £100 refund?

Not necessarily. The decision depends on policy, authority, customer circumstances, fraud controls and risk.

Give AI the minimum authority required to achieve its purpose safely.
RequestLikely approachReason
Store closing timeAILow-risk information.
Delivery statusAI, if authorisedRequires approved access.
RefundAI + policy / escalationDepends on authority.
Serious financial-loss complaintAI intake + humanComplex and higher risk.

7 · AI Practical Exercise

Customer Journey Detective

Paste this into your preferred generative AI tool:

I want you to act as a customer-experience analyst. I will describe an organisation and its customer journey. Identify: 1. Major customer pain points. 2. Repetitive customer enquiries. 3. Repetitive employee tasks. 4. Activities that could potentially be supported by an AI agent. 5. Activities that should remain human-led. 6. Information the agent would need. 7. Risks that should be considered. Do not recommend automation simply because something is technically possible. Prioritise customer value, safety, accuracy and human judgement. Organisation: [DESCRIBE YOUR ORGANISATION]

Then challenge the recommendations

Now challenge your recommendations. For every proposed AI-agent use case, explain: - what could go wrong; - what information could be wrong; - what authority is required; - when a human should take over; - what should never be automated. Rank the use cases from lowest risk to highest risk.
