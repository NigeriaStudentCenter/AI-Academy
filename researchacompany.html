Research a Company Before an Interview

Beginner~15 minCopilot · Gemini · Perplexity · ChatGPT
What you'll be able to do: walk into any interview with an AI-built brief on the company, current talking points, and sharp questions to ask — so you sound genuinely informed.
The scenario: Maya has an interview tomorrow and knows almost nothing about the company beyond the advert. Without research she'll sound generic. A 10-minute AI brief makes her sound like she already belongs there.
Use any free app: these prompts work in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot — pick whichever you like. Everything here works on the free version.

2Build a one-page company brief

Get the essentials in one place.

Research [company name] and give me a one-page brief: what they do, their main products or services, their mission and values, roughly their size, any recent news, and their main competitors. Use current sources and include the links.
What a good result looks like: a tidy summary with a few links - what they do, who they compete with, and something recent you can mention.
Refine it: reply — "Focus more on the team/department I'm interviewing for: [name it]."

3Find fresh talking points

Recent news shows you did your homework.

Based on [company]'s recent news or announcements, give me 3 specific things I could mention in my interview to show I've done my research, and why each matters to them.
What a good result looks like: e.g. "They just opened a Manchester office - I could ask how the role supports that growth."
Refine it: reply — "Turn each into a natural sentence I could actually say."

4Prepare smart questions to ask

Great candidates ask great questions.

Suggest 5 thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer about [company/role] that show genuine research and interest - not generic ones.
What a good result looks like: questions tied to their work or news, e.g. "How does this team measure success in the first six months?"
Refine it: reply — "Make two of them about the company's direction and culture."
Always verify: AI can be out of date or wrong. Double-check key facts (names, figures, recent news) on the company's own website before your interview.
Try this today (5 min): pick a real company you'd like to work for and run the one-page brief prompt. In three minutes you'll know more than most candidates walking in.
Common mistake: reciting a Wikipedia-style summary. Interviewers want to see you connect their work to the role - and that you checked the facts.
Privacy & ethics: you're researching public information about a company - that's fine. Just don't rely on a single AI answer; confirm anything important.

Check your understanding

Q1. Why use a web-searching tool for this, not just any chatbot?

You need current, real information - a tool with live web access (Copilot, Gemini, Perplexity).

Q2. What should you always do with AI research facts?

Verify them on the company's own site - AI can be outdated or wrong.

Q3. Why prepare questions to ask the interviewer?

Thoughtful, researched questions show genuine interest and set you apart.
